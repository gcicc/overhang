What counts as AGI?

There is no single objective definition of AGI. The ones below range from a mathematical formula nobody can compute, to a scored test battery, to a contract that leaves the call to a panel. When someone says AGI has arrived, the first question is which of these they mean; most such claims name none.

1950 Alan Turing Behavioural test
“I believe that in about fifty years' time it will be possible, to programme computers, with a storage capacity of about 109, to make them play the imitation game so well that an average interrogator will not have more than 70 per cent chance of making the right identification after five minutes of questioning.”
source · quote check: verbatim · “109” is 10^9 in the original; the superscript is lost in the source text. Proposed as a test of whether machines can think, not of general intelligence. Judges, duration and pass mark vary from one run to the next.
 Imitation game: can a judge tell machine from human in conversation? partly
2007-12 Shane Legg and Marcus Hutter Mathematical definition
“These are then mathematically formalised to produce a general measure of intelligence for arbitrary machines.”
source · quote check: verbatim · The most formally precise definition, but the sum runs over every computable environment, so it cannot be computed in practice.
 An agent's expected performance summed over all computable environments, weighted toward simpler ones. no
2018-04 OpenAI Charter Economic
“highly autonomous systems that outperform humans at most economically valuable work”
source · quote check: verbatim
 No test named; would need a measure of 'most economically valuable work'. partly
2019-11 François Chollet Learning efficiency
“describing intelligence as skill-acquisition efficiency”
source · quote check: verbatim · Defines intelligence, not AGI as such; ARC was built to measure it.
 ARC-AGI puzzles (scores in Measured on this page). yes
2023-11 Google DeepMind (Morris et al.) Levels framework
“This framework introduces levels of AGI performance, generality, and autonomy”
source · quote check: verbatim · Treats AGI as a ladder of levels rather than one threshold.
 Rates systems by depth (performance) and breadth (generality); no fixed test battery. partly
2023-12 Manifold markets plotted on this page Prediction-market criterion
“AGI can theoretically perform any intellectual task that a human being can”
source · quote check: verbatim · The four AGI markets in Crowd vs. CEOs share this wording. Their odds are odds on a moderator's judgement, not on a test result.
 A market moderator judges whether a system qualifies. no
2025-10 Dan Hendrycks, Dawn Song et al. Psychometric score
“defining AGI as matching the cognitive versatility and proficiency of a well-educated adult ... The resulting AGI scores (e.g., GPT-4 at 27%, GPT-5 at 57%)”
source · quote check: verbatim · The closest to an objective, scored definition. Scores in the quote are the paper's own, as of its writing.
 Ten cognitive domains from Cattell-Horn-Carroll theory, scored with adapted human test batteries; 100% = AGI. yes
2025-10 OpenAI and Microsoft contract Procedural
“Once AGI is declared by OpenAI, that declaration will now be verified by an independent expert panel.”
source · quote check: verbatim · Both companies' announcements of the 2026-04-27 amendment do not mention AGI. Secondary reports say the AGI clause was dropped then; that is not confirmed by either company.
 Declared by OpenAI, verified by a panel; no public criterion. no

"Measurable" means the definition names a test that gives a score anyone can rerun. "Partly" means it names what to measure but not how. Quotes are verbatim and re-checked weekly with the claims. Declarations that AGI has arrived, such as Jensen Huang's in September 2026, are in the claims ledger, not here: a declaration is not a definition.

Crowd vs. CEOs

Prediction-market odds that AGI arrives before each year (Manifold), set against the windows lab leaders have given. These are separate markets with their own resolution criteria, and none uses a CEO's definition of AGI. All four share one loose definition and resolve on a moderator's judgement (see What counts as AGI?). Read the line as the crowd's rough timing, not as a calibrated distribution.

Glossary

Every dotted-underlined term on the page shows its definition on hover. The same definitions, for touch screens:

AGI
Artificial general intelligence: roughly, AI that can do most cognitive work as well as a person. There is no single objective definition; see What counts as AGI?
ARC-AGI
Abstraction and Reasoning Corpus: grid puzzles that are easy for people and hard for AI, run by the ARC Prize Foundation.
capex
Capital expenditure: cash spent on long-lived assets such as buildings, servers and chips.
CC BY 4.0
Creative Commons Attribution licence: free to reuse with credit.
1e15
Scientific notation on chart axes: 1e15 means 10^15, a 1 followed by 15 zeros.
EDGAR
The SEC's public database of company filings.
Epoch AI
A research institute that tracks AI models, compute and benchmarks, and publishes its datasets openly.
FLOP
Floating-point operation, one arithmetic step on a computer. Training compute is the total number used to train a model.
FLOP/s
Floating-point operations per second: how fast a chip computes.
FP16/BF16
16-bit number formats. Fewer bits per number means faster arithmetic and less precision; 16-bit is the format every AI chip generation reports.
FP4
4-bit number format: faster still, least precise.
FP8
8-bit number format: faster than 16-bit, less precise.
FrontierMath
Epoch AI's benchmark of unpublished, research-level math problems. Tiers 1–3 are the less extreme levels; Tier 4 is the hardest.
GPQA Diamond
Graduate-level Google-Proof Q&A: the hardest subset of multiple-choice science questions written by PhD experts so that they cannot be answered by searching.
H100e
H100-equivalents: computing power expressed as the number of Nvidia H100 chips that would deliver the same throughput.
IFRS
International Financial Reporting Standards, the accounting rules most non-U.S. companies use.
Manifold
Manifold Markets, a prediction market where people bet play money on future events; prices read as probabilities.
METR
Model Evaluation & Threat Research, a nonprofit that measures how long a task AI agents can complete.
OLS
Ordinary least squares: the standard straight-line fit.
PFLOP/s
Peta-FLOP per second: 10^15 floating-point operations per second.
R²
Share of the variation the fitted line explains (1 = a perfect fit).
S&P 500
Index of 500 large U.S. companies' share prices, used as the market baseline.
SEC
U.S. Securities and Exchange Commission, which receives public companies' financial filings.
SWE-bench Verified
Software-engineering benchmark: fix real issues from open-source Python projects. Verified is the subset human engineers confirmed are solvable.
time horizon
The length of task (measured by how long it takes a skilled human) that a model completes successfully half the time.
TTM
Trailing twelve months: the most recent four quarters added together.
TWD
New Taiwan dollar.
UTC
Coordinated Universal Time (Greenwich time).
XBRL
eXtensible Business Reporting Language: machine-readable tags on the numbers in a filing.
